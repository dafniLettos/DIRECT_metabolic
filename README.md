## Code used for analyses in manuscript:

Michalettou et. al "Context-dependent molecular responses to metabolic disease relevant traits"

ABSTRACT

Metabolic diseases such as type 2 diabetes (T2D) arise through complex interactions between physiological, molecular, and environmental processes. However, most molecular studies examine traits such as age, sex, adiposity, and glycaemic status independently and assume that molecular relationships stay fixed throughout an individual's lifespan. Here, integrating transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, and genetic data from 3,027 individuals in the IMI DIRECT cohort, we show that these traits not only change the abundance of individual molecules, but also change how different molecules interact with one another. Interaction analyses revealed that the molecular effect of one trait frequently depends on the state of another, with sex-specific age effects being especially common. Moreover, by exploring the influence of metabolic disease traits on the relationships between different types of molecules, we demonstrated that cross-omic molecular coordination is itself dynamically remodelled by physiological and metabolic state.Causal inference further identified a directional network through which age effects propagate across omics layers. Collectively, our findings show that metabolic disease-relevant traits jointly reshape the architecture of cross-omic molecular networks, not merely their individual components, supporting a model in which metabolic disease susceptibility emerges through dynamic network rewiring. Our work implies that molecular disease mechanisms and biomarkers are context-dependent, providing a framework for more accurate precision approaches to disease prevention and treatment.
